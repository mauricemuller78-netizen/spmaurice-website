Real Madrid 2021/22: Champions of Europe

Carlo Ancelotti's 2021/22 Champions League-winning Real Madrid. A tactical analysis of the structures, player roles and decisive moments behind their European success.

A Flexible 4-3-3

Real Madrid's starting point was a 4-3-3, but Ancelotti gave his most important players considerable freedom within that structure. Rather than forcing the attack into rigid positional patterns, Madrid relied on intelligent movement, individual quality and the relationships between their experienced midfield and front three.

Benzema: The Reference Point

Karim Benzema was the central reference of the attack, but he was far more than a traditional number nine. He regularly dropped away from the defensive line to connect midfield and attack, and he also drifted frequently towards the left side. From there, he could combine directly with Vinícius, create overloads and help generate chances through quick combinations, rotations and movements around the opposition's left defensive channel. That Benzema–Vinícius relationship was one of Madrid's most important attacking mechanisms throughout the season.

Vinícius: Width, Depth and Freedom

Vinícius Júnior provided Madrid with width and directness on the left, but he was not restricted to the touchline. He could receive wide, attack the space behind the defence or move inside when Benzema dropped deeper. That relationship between the two became one of Madrid's most dangerous attacking mechanisms throughout the season.

Midfield Control

The midfield of Toni Kroos, Luka Modrić and Casemiro gave Madrid control, experience and balance. Kroos could drop into deeper areas to help the build-up and dictate the next pass, Modrić connected phases with his movement and ability to escape pressure, while Casemiro protected the structure behind them and allowed the other two to play with greater freedom.

Valverde and Rodrygo: Different Solutions

Federico Valverde and Rodrygo gave Ancelotti different solutions on the right. Valverde offered intensity, running power and defensive balance, while Rodrygo provided more attacking movement and threat around the final third. Their different profiles allowed Madrid to adjust the right side depending on the match and game state.

Out of Possession

Real Madrid were highly adaptable without the ball. Their defensive shape changed depending on the opponent, but they most often defended in a mid-block or low block, prioritising compactness and control of central areas rather than constant high pressing.

Flexible Defensive Structures

One of Madrid's most common defensive shapes resembled a 6-3-1. The two wingers dropped alongside the back four to form a line of six, while the three central midfielders stayed compact in front and Benzema remained as the highest player. This gave Madrid strong protection across the width of the pitch and made it difficult for opponents to find space close to the penalty area.

They could also fall into a 5-4-1. In those situations, either one of the wide players or one of the midfielders adjusted into the defensive line, while the remaining players formed a compact four ahead. The exact positioning depended on the opponent and where the threat was coming from.

Ancelotti was not tied to one defensive structure. Madrid could also defend in a 4-1-4-1 or a 4-4-2, again depending on the opposition and the phase of the game. The important principle was not the exact formation, but the ability to remain compact, protect central spaces and adapt the block to the opponent.

2022 Champions League Final — Real Madrid vs Liverpool

Real Madrid were dominated for large parts of the final, which reflected a broader theme from their Champions League campaign. Under Carlo Ancelotti, this team was not always the side that controlled matches best — but it repeatedly showed one of its greatest strengths: finding a way to win.

1 / 6

With the Ball — Trying to Escape Liverpool's Man-to-Man Press

Liverpool pressed Madrid aggressively in a man-to-man structure, making it difficult to build short from the back. Madrid responded with a few different adjustments in an attempt to create free players and progress through pressure.

Toni Kroos regularly dropped deeper to help the first phase of the build-up and offer an extra passing option. Luka Modrić changed his height depending on the situation, sometimes dropping towards the ball and at other times positioning himself higher to try to escape his marker and connect with the next line. These movements were attempts to create short combinations rather than simply accepting Liverpool's pressure and playing long every time.

Without the Ball — Low Block and Survival

Without possession, Madrid's approach was much more direct: drop into a low block, protect the box and survive Liverpool's pressure. Liverpool controlled territory and created far more attacking volume, while Madrid focused on remaining compact and limiting the quality of the final action as much as possible.

The numbers reflected the balance of the match. Liverpool generated roughly 3 xG, while Madrid finished below 1 xG. The decisive individual performance came from Thibaut Courtois, who repeatedly denied Liverpool and produced an extraordinary goalkeeping display.

Final Assessment

For me, this match perfectly represents Real Madrid's 2021/22 Champions League run. They were often second best in terms of control and chance creation, yet they still kept finding solutions in the moments that mattered. Against Liverpool, Courtois saved almost everything, Madrid absorbed huge pressure and then took the decisive opportunity when it arrived.

That was perhaps the defining quality of Ancelotti's team: they did not always need to dominate in order to win. They could suffer, adapt, survive and still produce the decisive moment. In this final, that ability was enough to make them champions of Europe again.
