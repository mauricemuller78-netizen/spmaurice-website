Manchester City 2022/23: Champions of Europe

Pep Guardiola's Champions League-winning Manchester City. A tactical analysis of the 2022/23 season.

SPMaurice2023 Champions League Winners

Tactical Identity

Manchester City typically started from a 4-3-3, but the formation on the teamsheet only told part of the story. Without the ball, Guardiola could organise his side into a 4-4-2 high press. In possession, the structure was far more fluid, with 3-2-4-1, 2-3-5 and 3-4-3 shapes appearing at different stages of the season.

Despite the changes in the build-up, several principles remained constant. The wingers provided width and stretched the opposition's defensive line, while the No. 8s occupied advanced positions in the half-spaces. This combination gave City both passing options between the lines and the space to attack down the outside. The evolving roles of the defenders were essential to maintaining that balance.

A Squad Built to Dominate

For me, what made this Manchester City side exceptional was the combination of Guardiola's tactical flexibility and an extraordinary squad. Rodri gave the entire system stability and control, while Erling Haaland supplied a level of goalscoring threat that made him, in my view, a genuine Ballon d'Or contender. Kevin De Bruyne remained one of the world's most dangerous chance creators, capable of finding decisive passes from central areas or the wings.

Even the depth was remarkable. Phil Foden offered another elite attacking option, while Julián Álvarez brought quality, movement and versatility. City could change individual players without losing the overall level of the team. It was not only an outstanding starting eleven: the strength of the squad made Guardiola's different tactical solutions possible.

Rodri's importance became even clearer in the period after the Champions League triumph. From 5 February 2023 to 25 May 2024, he went 74 Manchester City appearances without defeat (counting matches decided by a penalty shoot-out as draws), and he later won the 2024 Ballon d'Or. Those achievements extended beyond the 2022/23 season, but they underline the quality of the player at the heart of this team.

First Half of the Season — The Cancelo Variations

During the first half of the 2022/23 season, while João Cancelo was still part of the squad, Guardiola frequently used two different structures in possession: a 2-3-5 and a 3-4-3 diamond.

In the 2-3-5, Cancelo and Kyle Walker moved inside from their full-back positions to join Rodri in midfield, creating a three-man platform behind the attack. Ahead of them, two players occupied advanced positions in the half-spaces, while the attacking line stretched the opposition across the pitch.

The alternative was a 3-4-3 diamond. Walker moved into the back line as a third centre-back, allowing Cancelo to advance towards the left wing or left half-space. Kevin De Bruyne operated nominally as the No. 10, but regularly drifted towards either flank, where he could find space and create chances from wide areas.

Second Half of the Season — Stones as the Inverted Centre-Back

In the second half of the season, Guardiola increasingly built his team around a back four made up predominantly of natural centre-backs. In possession, this often transformed into a 3-2-4-1: three defenders remained behind the ball, while John Stones stepped forward to play alongside Rodri as a second holding midfielder.

What made this system especially fascinating was Stones' libero-like role. Rather than simply acting as a conventional centre-back, he moved into midfield to help City control central spaces and progress the ball. It was an unusual interpretation of the libero idea in a modern positional structure, and one of the defining tactical features of City's Champions League-winning run.

Champions League Final — Manchester City vs Inter Milan

Manchester City faced Inter Milan in the 2023 Champions League final. The video below provides the basis for a closer tactical examination of the match.

Champions League Final — Breaking Down Inter's Man-to-Man Press

Manchester City approached the final with a 3-4-3 structure. Initially, İlkay Gündoğan occupied the central No. 10 position, but Kevin De Bruyne's injury forced Guardiola to adapt. Phil Foden came on and moved into the central role, while Gündoğan operated more frequently in the left half-space.

John Stones, who started at right-back, played an exceptionally attacking role and regularly pushed into the right half-space. With Gündoğan on the left and Stones on the right, both players sometimes drifted so wide that they were almost on the touchline. This was a response to Inter's aggressive man-to-man pressing: by stretching their markers and rotating positions, City tried to open space inside for Foden to receive and threaten between the lines.

That constant positional rotation was also central to City's winning goal. Their movement repeatedly forced Inter's defenders to adjust, eventually creating the space for Rodri at the edge of the penalty area, where he finished the decisive attack.

Match Assessment

For me, this final was another demonstration of why Pep Guardiola is the greatest tactical coach of all time. Whatever Inter tried to take away, City kept searching for an answer. Guardiola's positional adjustments and the players' rotations allowed his team to generate space, create overloads and find opportunities even against such an aggressive defensive approach.
