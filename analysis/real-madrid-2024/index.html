Real Madrid 2023/24: Champions of Europe

An analysis of Carlo Ancelotti's Champions League-winning Real Madrid side, beginning with the team's in-possession structure and the roles that gave it such flexibility.

Ancelotti's Diamond and Christmas-Tree Principles

In possession, Madrid's shape often resembled a 4-1-2-1-2 or 4-3-1-2, drawing on principles from Carlo Ancelotti's famous Christmas-tree systems of earlier eras. Rather than treating the formation as a fixed arrangement, Madrid used it as a starting point for highly flexible positional relationships.

Bellingham: A Midfielder in a Forward's Position

One of the most unusual elements was Jude Bellingham's role. Although he frequently occupied a forward position, he was not a conventional striker. During build-up, he continued to behave like a midfielder — effectively a number 10 or number 8 — dropping into deeper areas to receive, link play and help Madrid progress the ball. But as the attack reached the penalty area, he became a genuine goal threat, arriving and finishing like a forward. His role connected the midfield and attacking lines without forcing him to play as a traditional number 9.

Vinícius and Rodrygo: Freedom Across the Front Line

Vinícius Júnior and Rodrygo formed the more natural attacking pair, but neither was restricted to a narrow central position. Both enjoyed exceptional freedom to move and often pulled wide during build-up. At times, they even appeared together on the left, changing Madrid's attacking relationships and making their movements difficult to predict or mark.

Valverde: Width and Balance on the Right

Federico Valverde frequently occupied a very wide, high position on Madrid's right. Sometimes he advanced beyond Dani Carvajal; at other moments, he dropped into or protected the space Carvajal left behind. His mobility meant Madrid could create width and height on the right while still maintaining the balance needed to support their attacks.

Kroos: Control From the Left of the Build-Up

On the left, Toni Kroos regularly dropped deeper, often forming what effectively became a back three in possession. That movement gave him the time, space and composure to dictate the next pass. From those deeper zones, his extraordinary passing quality and game intelligence allowed Madrid to control the rhythm of their build-up and find solutions across the pitch.
